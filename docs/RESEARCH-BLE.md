# Advertising flood wins; GATT pulls firmware

Build the proof of concept as a custom NimBLE advertising flood, not as Bluetooth SIG mesh and not as a network of GATT connections. Every board broadcasts its own state (colour, holding bit, firmware version, dev-mode bit) in a legacy manufacturer-data advertisement every **20 ms**, scans passively and continuously, and renders the additive mix of every board currently advertising "holding" (itself included); the holding board's own screen changes on the next LVGL frame and the others follow within one advertising event. With no Wi-Fi on the shared radio the only loss mechanism left is advertising collisions among the boards, and the collision model puts the first event of a change on air within **30 ms** and all nine peers of a ten-board room updated with 99% probability within about **150 ms** (60 ms at three boards), inside the 30-85 ms band that users judge "instant" and under the 100-150 ms point where perceived quality drops. SIG mesh (ESP-BLE-MESH) runs on the same advertising bearer, so it brings no latency gain, but it caps an unsegmented vendor message at **8 bytes**, blocks its sender for **90 ms per message**, needs a firmware provisioner or Espressif's test-only self-provisioning API when there is no phone, and has a documented record of fighting any GATT server you run beside it. GATT connections give acknowledged 7.5 ms hops but at ten boards demand 45 links or a hub election on a chip whose host allows 9 connections, whose controller allows 10 activities including the scanner, and for which Espressif ships no symmetric multi-role example. The firmware requirement is met by a second mode of the same NimBLE stack: a board in dev mode advertises connectable with its version and the id of the peer it wants the image from; that peer connects as central and streams the ~2 MB image with Write Without Response at MTU 512, DLE 251 and a 7.5 ms interval, which Espressif's own throughput example documents at **~500 kbps on 1M PHY (about 34 s)** and Espressif's 2026 follow-up at up to **1.38 Mbps on 2M PHY (about 12 s)**; `esp_ota_end` verifies the appended SHA-256 and app rollback gives the new image one boot to prove itself. Estimated cost is +250-350 KB of flash against 1.16 MB of OTA-slot headroom and roughly 45-75 KB of internal RAM. Every open risk is measurable in a day on the three boards: whether the S3 controller accepts 20 ms non-connectable advertising, how it interleaves its own advertising with an open scan window, whether the host keeps up with 500 reports per second, and what the transfer rate is while LVGL runs.

## Eighty-five milliseconds, an empty radio and no phone set the bar

Three perception numbers define "lowest latency" for this toy. Kaaresoja, Brewster and Lantz measured the point of subjective simultaneity for visual feedback to a touch at **32 ms**, recommend visual feedback within **30-85 ms**, and found perceived quality dropping significantly between **100 and 150 ms** ([Glasgow eprints 104653](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/104653/)); Nielsen's 0.1 s limit for a system that feels like it reacts instantaneously agrees ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Pavlovych and Stuerzlinger found that latency and dropouts hurt performance while moderate jitter does not ([SFU VVISE](https://vvise.iat.sfu.ca/pubs/pavlovych2010effects)). The interaction here is a discrete hold, not dragging, so the 6-11 ms dragging thresholds in the touch literature do not apply. The engineering target is therefore a remote screen that changes within ~85 ms median and 150 ms worst case, a holder's colour that never flickers out while the finger stays down, and a release that clears within the same budget; redundancy matters more than raw speed.

Removing Wi-Fi removes the largest uncertainty in the research. The ESP32-S3 has one 2.4 GHz radio; with a station connected, Wi-Fi and BLE each get **50% of a coexistence period longer than 100 ms**, scan windows are cut short by the Wi-Fi slice, and only one in every N advertising events may pre-empt Wi-Fi ([coexist.rst:185-214](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/coexist.rst)); one S3 user reported BLE loss rising from ~3% to "20%+, with some long streaks of 100% loss" once Wi-Fi was active ([esp32.com p=126397](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=126397), forum snippet). None of that applies to a Wi-Fi-free build, so advertising loss comes only from collisions among the boards and from the scanner's own channel hopping. The same constraint settles ESP-NOW: it is a Wi-Fi feature, "ESP-NOW data must be transmitted after Wi-Fi is started" ([esp_now.rst:84](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-reference/network/esp_now.rst)), so its sub-10 ms round trips ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2024/02/11/benchmarking-latency-across-common-wireless-links-for-mcus), [esp-now #115](https://github.com/espressif/esp-now/issues/115)) are only the reference ceiling the Bluetooth design does not need to reach. Nothing else is on the chip: the S3 is Bluetooth 5.0 LE only, no Classic, no 802.15.4 ([overview.rst:8-14, 138-140](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/ble/overview.rst), [soc_caps.h:596-600](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/soc/esp32s3/include/soc/soc_caps.h)).

No phone changes two things. SIG mesh needs a provisioner, which without an app means a firmware provisioner on one board or a self-provisioning path (next section). And every BLE firmware-update building block the researchers found, from Espressif's `ble_ota` component to h2zero/nimbleota and kodira/simple_ble_ota, is a receiver whose sender is a phone, PC or browser; no ESP32-to-ESP32 BLE firmware transfer project exists in the open ([components.espressif.com ble_ota](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/ble_ota), [kodira/simple_ble_ota](https://components.espressif.com/components/kodira/simple_ble_ota/versions/0.2.0/readme), [h2zero/nimbleota](https://github.com/h2zero/nimbleota)). The board-to-board firmware path has to be written on plain NimBLE GATT, which makes "does this stack leave room for a GATT link" a first-order selection criterion rather than an afterthought.

## Mesh serialises every send into 90 ms and cannot share the radio with your OTA service

ESP-BLE-MESH is Zephyr's mesh stack on top of the same advertising bearer the custom flood would use. Every Network PDU goes out as a non-connectable legacy advertisement, and the stack's single advertising task starts advertising, sleeps for `adv_cnt × (adv_int + 10)` ms and stops before taking the next queued message ([adv.c:53-66, 188-198](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/core/adv.c)). With the examples' `ESP_BLE_MESH_TRANSMIT(2, 20)` that is **90 ms of bearer time per message** by Espressif's own macro comment, a ceiling of about eleven messages per second per node ([esp_ble_mesh_defs.h:149-164](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/api/esp_ble_mesh_defs.h)); receivers scan continuously at a 20 ms window equal to the interval ([scan.c:34-58](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/core/scan.c)). Latency in a one-room network with TTL 0 is therefore the physics of the flood plus stack overhead. Espressif's own ESP32 vendor-model round trips at 1 m were mostly **~22 ms with a 157 ms worst case** ([vendor_client README:19-42](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/esp_ble_mesh/vendor_models/vendor_client/README.md)); on nRF52840 hardware a single hop measured a median of 4.54 ms, mean 7.86 ms and max 111 ms ([ar5iv 2310.14681](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.14681)), and with three and seven nodes publishing concurrently the one-way mean/90th percentile were 24.9/50.4 ms and 37.9/79.6 ms ([ar5iv 2106.04230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04230)). Those numbers fit the target, with the seven-sender tail already touching it.

Mesh fails this project on payload, provisioning and radio ownership. An unsegmented access message carries 11 bytes, **8 after the 3-byte vendor opcode** ([ble-mesh-faq.rst 2.8](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/esp-ble-mesh/ble-mesh-faq.rst)); RGB plus a holding flag plus a version already fills it, and the first byte beyond that triggers segmentation, which roughly triples latency (24.9 to 76.3 ms mean in the 19-byte test, [ar5iv 2106.04230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04230)) and brings four-retry reassembly, so the "more bandwidth for effects later" path is closed. Provisioning without a phone is the second wall: the public node API refuses every local key, binding or subscription call with `-EIO NotProvisioned` until an external provisioner has run ([local.c:220-223, 292-295, 354-357](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/core/local.c)); the only fixed-key path in v5.5.4 is `bt_mesh_device_auto_enter_network()` in `core/test.c`, gated by `CONFIG_BLE_MESH_SELF_TEST`, whose underlying `bt_mesh_provision()` is documented as "should normally not be used directly by the application ... except for testing purposes" ([main.h:607-624](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/core/include/mesh/main.h), [test.c:32-136](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/core/test.c)), and whose AppKey binding and group subscription are RAM-only and lost at power-off ([ble-mesh-faq.rst 1.16](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/esp-ble-mesh/ble-mesh-faq.rst)). The supported alternative is a firmware Provisioner on one board, modelled on the 595-line `vendor_client` example, with at least three acknowledged Config Client exchanges per joining node, a **4 s default response timeout** and a 5 s unprovisioned-beacon interval ([vendor_client main.c:164-202, 343-452](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/esp_ble_mesh/vendor_models/vendor_client/main/main.c), [Kconfig.in:1252-1261, 482-492](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/Kconfig.in)). The third wall is the firmware link: the mesh stack owns advertising, and running your own NimBLE GATT server beside it is a documented sore point: "when both are enabled, only one of them functions correctly" (#14413, closed with "Resolution: NA"), extended advertising broke mesh until a service-registration ordering trick was found (#17663), and GAP adverts started beside mesh reported success but never transmitted (#17564) ([#14413](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/14413), [#17663](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/17663), [#17564](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/17564)). v5.5.1 added mesh-native DFU ("Support Device Firmware Update", [v5.5.1 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/espressif/esp-idf/release/v5.5.1)), but the whole Mesh 1.1 code base is "a preview version" ([esp-ble-mesh.rst:4-6](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-reference/bluetooth/esp-ble-mesh.rst)). Two further pitfalls: with `CONFIG_BLE_MESH_SETTINGS` the default `SEQ_STORE_RATE 0` writes the sequence number to NVS on every single message ([settings.c:1719-1729](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/core/storage/settings.c)), and an open ESP32-S3 issue reports `btc_task_post` blocking forever when a model sends from inside a mesh callback under flood ([#16883](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/16883)). Mesh is also the heaviest option, adding a 3 KB advertising task stack, a 5 KB host stack and 60 advertising buffers in internal RAM and an estimated +450-700 KB of flash; no Espressif S3 measurement exists for either ([kernel.h:46](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/esp_ble_mesh/common/include/mesh/kernel.h), [NimBLE Kconfig.in:58-64](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/Kconfig.in)).

## GATT links hop in 7.5 ms but ten boards mean 45 links and an election

Connections are the only option with link-layer acknowledgement, and a notification is the fastest hop on paper. The connection-interval floor is 7.5 ms ([ble-connection.rst:41-54](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/ble/get-started/ble-connection.rst)); a Nordic engineer places a notification at **1 to 7.5 ms** after it is queued at that interval ([DevZone 68990](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/68990/sending-packet-latency-over-ble)); a star forwards in two hops, a full mesh in one. NimBLE's defaults when you pass no parameters are a 30-50 ms interval and a 2.56 s supervision timeout ([ble_gap.h:113-124](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/include/host/ble_gap.h)). Loss surfaces as a supervision-timeout disconnect rather than a silently missed packet, which is the argument Espressif's own staff make for connections when zero drops are required ([esp32.com p=126397](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=126397), snippet).

The S3's hard limits bite at ten boards. The host allows **1-9 connections** (`CONFIG_BT_NIMBLE_MAX_CONNECTIONS`, default 3) and the controller **1-10 activities** (`CONFIG_BT_CTRL_BLE_MAX_ACT`, 828 bytes each) covering every connection, the scanner and each advertising set ([NimBLE Kconfig.in:275-289](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/Kconfig.in), [esp32c3/Kconfig.in:5-13](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/controller/esp32c3/Kconfig.in)), so a full mesh of ten boards (nine links each) leaves no activity for scanning, and a design that keeps both advertising and scanning caps at eight links. Espressif's `ble_multi_conn` example, the one that schedules many links, **does not build for the S3**: "the ESP32/ESP32-C3/ESP32-S3/ESP32-C2 does not support the relevant vendor HCI" ([ble_multi_conn_cent README:1-8](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/ble_multi_conn/ble_multi_conn_cent/README.md)). The S3 multi-role example is `ble_gattc_gatts_coex`, one link per role, whose README states that "the actual ble coex stability is not the criteria for this demo example" ([README:1-14](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/ble_gattc_gatts_coex/README.md)). Connection setup is serialised: `ble_gap_connect()` is busy while another connect is in progress and, by default, while scanning ([ble_gap.c:8071-8105](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_gap.c)); the option meant to lift that, `BT_NIMBLE_HOST_ALLOW_CONNECT_WITH_SCAN`, has an S3 report of the scan receiving nothing once a connection starts ([#15258](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/15258)). Espressif's only multi-link sizing rule, from the C6/H2 example, is 5 ms of air time per link with interval = links × 5 ms ([ble_multi_conn_cent main.c:18-20, 394-405](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/ble_multi_conn/ble_multi_conn_cent/main/main.c)); at nine links that is 45-50 ms, and a two-hop star at that interval already sits around 100 ms. No symmetric "every node is central and peripheral" example exists from Espressif or the community (the connections researcher searched for it and found only ESP-BLE-MESH docs and 2016-17 Bluedroid threads), so hub election, reconnection after a 2.56 s timeout and link bookkeeping are all new code with no reference; a chain of ten at 30 ms is ~270 ms worst case. For three boards (three links) a connection mesh is workable, and its one unambiguous strength is the firmware transfer, which is just a GATT stream on a link that already exists. It is the right building block for the firmware link and the wrong one for the state fan-out at ten boards.

The table condenses the comparison on the six criteria the question asked for; the flood column is argued in the next section.

| Criterion | SIG mesh (ESP-BLE-MESH) | Custom advertising flood (NimBLE) | GATT connections (NimBLE) | ESP-NOW (reference only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provisioning / setup | NetKey, AppKey, unicast addresses, bindings; firmware provisioner (~600 lines, 3-4 acked exchanges, 4 s timeouts) or test-only self-provisioning with RAM-only bindings | None: BLE address is the identity, manufacturer data is the message | Discovery, role election, serialised connects (0.1-1 s each), reconnect state machine; no reference design on S3 | None, but requires Wi-Fi started; excluded |

| RAM / flash (estimates, no S3 measurement) | +20-40 KB internal over a GATT baseline, +450-700 KB flash | ~45-60 KB internal, ~11-21 KB of it PSRAM-eligible; +150-250 KB flash | +828 B/activity, ~1 KB host per link; +25-40 KB at 9 links; +250-350 KB flash | n/a |

| Complexity / known issues | Largest stack, preview v1.1, #16883 deadlock on S3, #17549 C++ break, relay storms | ~300 lines; silent report discard, undocumented adv/scan interleaving | 300-400 lines plus election; `ble_multi_conn` not for S3; #15258 | Requires Wi-Fi |

## A 20 ms flood updates ten boards within 150 ms with nothing to provision

The flood has no setup because the BLE address is the identity and the manufacturer-data AD structure is the message. `ble_hs_util_ensure_addr(0)` followed by `ble_hs_id_infer_auto(0, &own_addr_type)` yields the public eFuse address, and with privacy off every scan report carries the sender's stable identity address in `desc->addr` ([addr.c:93-114](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/util/src/addr.c), [ble_hs_id.h:95-122](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/include/host/ble_hs_id.h), [ble_gap.h:503-530](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/include/host/ble_gap.h)). Twenty-millisecond advertising is legal in this stack: the host rejects only `itvl_min < 0x20` (20 ms) regardless of advertising type, the pre-5.0 `BLE_HCI_ADV_ITVL_NONCONN_MIN` (100 ms) constant is defined but referenced nowhere in the host, and Espressif's feature table lists the Bluetooth 5.0 "High Duty Cycle Non-Connectable Advertising" feature as supported by the ESP32-S3 controller ([ble_gap.c:4140-4152](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_gap.c), [hci_common.h:1751-1754](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/include/nimble/hci_common.h), [ble-feature-support-status.rst:94-113](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.rst)); the getting-started guide states the range as "20 ms to 10.24 s" ([ble-device-discovery.rst:114](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/ble/get-started/ble-device-discovery.rst)). Advertising data is replaced in place with `ble_gap_adv_set_data()`, which performs no advertising-state check ([ble_gap.c:4343-4431](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_gap.c)), so a touch costs one HCI command and no stop/start. On the receive side, `ble_gap_disc()` with `passive = 1`, `filter_duplicates = 0`, window equal to interval and `BLE_HS_FOREVER` is continuous listening ([ble_gap.h:367-389](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/include/host/ble_gap.h), [ble_gap.c:7151-7158](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_gap.c)); NimBLE omits the Flags AD when `flags == 0` and delivers every report in its default observer mode, so a 31-byte PDU minus the 2-byte AD header and 2-byte company ID leaves **27 bytes** of payload ([ble_hs_adv.c:285-301](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_hs_adv.c), [ble_hs_adv.h:31-34](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/include/host/ble_hs_adv.h)).

Reliability has no acknowledgement, so it is bought with repetition, and the arithmetic favours a fast interval in a quiet room. In a shielded box a continuously scanning nRF54L15 received **96-99% of advertisements** from a single advertiser, the loss coming from the scanner's own channel-hop gaps ([DevZone 126337](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/126337/benchmark-for-ble-receiver-performance/558157)); Kindt et al. showed over 29,100 runs that with window equal to interval the worst-case discovery latency is bounded by one advertising interval ([ar5iv 1509.04366](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.04366)), to which the Link Layer adds a mandatory 0-10 ms random advDelay per event ([DevZone 85629](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/85629/nature-of-pseudo-random-delay-advdelay-0-10-ms-added-to-the-advertising-interval)). Multiple advertisers collide: the published model gives a per-advertisement success of roughly (1 − 2ρ)^(N−1) for N advertisers each loading the channel by ρ ([NSF-hosted paper, snippet](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10275622)); deployments with more than 200 tags still kept acceptable reception at collision rates of 0.22-0.33 because advertisements repeat ([Nikodem & Bawiec](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/s20010107?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,venue,citationCount)); and the capture effect adds about 3% at 14 devices ([HAL hal-03165168](https://hal-imt-atlantique.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03165168)). Applying that model to a full 47-octet legacy PDU (376 µs on air at 1 Mb/s, [BlueFlood](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2002.12906)) with the 5 ms mean advDelay, the flood researcher derived the idealised figures below (inference, single room, no interference, the receiver's own transmit time counted as one more contender):

| Advertising interval | Per-event loss, N=3 / 5 / 10 | Time until all N−1 peers have the new state with 99% probability, N=3 / 5 / 10 |
|---|---|---|
| 20 ms | 5.9% / 11.5% / 24% | 60 ms / 90 ms / 150 ms |
| 50 ms | 2.7% / 5.4% / 11.7% | 120 ms / 180 ms / 240 ms |
| 100 ms | 1.4% / 2.8% / 6.3% | 220 ms / 220 ms / 330 ms |

The channel occupancy at ten boards on 20 ms is only about 15% per advertising channel; the loss is pairwise overlap, not saturation, and the only way to buy diversity is more events, because a scanner dwelling on one channel gets one reception opportunity per three-channel event, not three. A 20 ms interval therefore meets the target with margin at ten boards; 100 ms does not. The notes expected real loss 1.5-3× the table under Wi-Fi; without Wi-Fi the remaining unknowns are the S3 controller's own interleaving of its advertising events with an open scan window (undocumented; the controller ships as a prebuilt library under `components/bt/controller/lib_esp32c3_family/esp32s3/`) and the controller's silent discard of advertising reports once **100 unprocessed ones** are queued, a vendor event (`0xF0`) that the NimBLE host only logs and never surfaces to the application ([esp32c3/Kconfig.in:232-264](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/controller/esp32c3/Kconfig.in), [ble_hs_hci.c:672-688](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_hs_hci.c)). The mitigations are a microsecond-scale `BLE_GAP_EVENT_DISC` callback that copies 16 bytes into a FreeRTOS queue and `CONFIG_BT_NIMBLE_HOST_QUEUE_CONG_CHECK=y`, which exists for exactly the case "scanning and scan duplicate is not enabled ... adv packets will be lost when host queue is congested" instead of exhausting controller memory ([NimBLE Kconfig.in:266-273](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/Kconfig.in)). The one documented scan-stall bug, a continuous 30/30 ms scan silently stopping after hours at ~50 advertisements per second, was fixed in the controller in July 2020 and no S3 report from 2023-2026 was found ([#4196](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/4196)); the one open S3 controller issue of that shape (#19297, scanning and advertising silently stopping, worse under load) is specific to `CONFIG_BT_CTRL_RUN_IN_FLASH_ONLY=y`, so that option stays off ([#19297](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/19297)).

Growth is where the flood and mesh part ways. Fourteen of the 27 bytes are spare after the state fields proposed below, and the design patterns of every working lossy-broadcast system say to spend them on parameters, not frames: game netcode sends state with sequence numbers so the newest snapshot supersedes lost ones and keeps running on the last input rather than waiting ([Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/state_synchronization/)); the ESP-NOW badge swarm broadcasts a shared clock every ~5 ms, adopts the highest value seen, and renders effects as a stateless function of shared time quantised to 50 ms because at 10 ms "network jitter started to dominate" ([Hackaday.io 205611](https://hackaday.io/project/205611-self-synchronising-led-badges-using-esp-now)); PixMob wristbands render per-band randomness from a seed carried in the packet and depend on repetition rather than acknowledgement ([pixmob reverse engineering](https://github.com/danielweidman/pixmob-ir-reverse-engineering)). Varying intensity is one byte; a cycling pattern is a pattern id, speed and phase rendered from a shared 16-bit millisecond clock, at zero extra bandwidth per frame. If bytes ever run out, the S3 supports extended advertising with 251 bytes per HCI data command, up to 1650 bytes per set, four instances and a 2M secondary PHY ([NimBLE Kconfig.in:591-659](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/Kconfig.in), [ble-device-discovery.rst:67-105](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/ble/get-started/ble-device-discovery.rst)), at the price of moving the whole application to the `ble_gap_ext_adv_*` API and `BLE_GAP_EVENT_EXT_DISC` (the legacy calls return `BLE_HS_ENOTSUP` once `CONFIG_BT_NIMBLE_EXT_ADV=y`, [ble_gap.c:4213, 4346, 4405](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_gap.c)) and of two-PDU receptions that keep a scanner busy longer; and the GATT link built for firmware is there for anything bulk. No published BLE-advertising colour swarm with measured latency exists; the hobby swarms chose ESP-NOW for the same pairing-free-broadcast reason, which is the pattern this design reproduces on Bluetooth.

## Recommended architecture: flood for state, one on-demand GATT link for firmware

### Modes, roles and who connects to whom

Every board runs the same firmware with two modes. In normal mode it advertises **non-connectable** (`BLE_GAP_CONN_MODE_NON`, `BLE_GAP_DISC_MODE_NON`, no Flags AD) and scans; it never accepts connections. In dev mode it advertises **connectable** (`BLE_GAP_CONN_MODE_UND`) with the dev bit set and a `target` byte naming the peer it wants firmware from; it chooses that peer from the versions it already sees in the flood (highest version, ties broken by lowest address). Only the named peer reacts: it cancels its scan, connects as central, restarts its scan after `BLE_GAP_EVENT_CONNECT`, and streams its running image into the dev board with Write Without Response. The dev board controls the transfer (it sends START, acknowledges sectors, verifies and reboots), so it "pulls" in every sense that matters, while the bytes flow central to peripheral for two reasons: Espressif's throughput example documents **Write Without Response at ~500 kbps against Notify at ~340 kbps** on the same 1M PHY settings ([throughput_app README:9-15](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/throughput_app/README.md), [blecent_throughput README:142](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/throughput_app/blecent_throughput/README.md)), and a legacy connectable advertisement ends when a central connects (every peripheral example restarts advertising from its connect/disconnect handlers, [bleprph main.c:253-310](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/bleprph/main/main.c)), whereas `ble_gap_connect()` does not touch the initiator's own advertising ([ble_gap.c:8071-8105](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/src/ble_gap.c)), so the serving board's colour state keeps flooding during the 15-40 s transfer and only the dev board (which is busy showing a progress bar anyway) drops out. Activities: one advertising set, one scanner, one connection, well inside the default `BT_CTRL_BLE_MAX_ACT` of 6; `CONFIG_BT_NIMBLE_MAX_CONNECTIONS=2` leaves one spare. If the literal direction (dev board as central, `Notify` stream) is preferred, every board must advertise connectable at all times and restart advertising on every connect, which costs the 3-byte Flags AD, exposes every board to stray connections, and uses the slower documented direction; keep it as the fallback.

### Advertising parameters and payload layout

Advertise with `itvl_min = itvl_max = 0x20` (**20 ms**), default channel map (all three channels), `own_addr_type` from `ble_hs_id_infer_auto(0, ...)`, duration `BLE_HS_FOREVER`. For the three-board proof of concept keep 20 ms fixed and never touch the interval; from about six boards upward adopt the Trickle-like "burst then settle" the flood note derived from the mesh and RFC 6206 patterns: 20 ms for ~300 ms after any local change (about ten events, five reception opportunities for every peer at N=10), then 0xA0 (**100 ms**) steady state, accepting that an interval change needs `ble_gap_adv_stop()` and `ble_gap_adv_start()` because the interval lives in the parameters command ([Zephyr mesh Kconfig](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zephyrproject-rtos/zephyr/main/subsys/bluetooth/mesh/Kconfig), [RFC 6206](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6206.html)). Update the data in place with `ble_gap_adv_set_data(buf, len)` on a prebuilt 31-byte buffer; bump `seq` only when the state changes.

| Offset | Bytes | Field | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| 1 | 1 | AD type | 0xFF manufacturer specific |
| 2-3 | 2 | Company ID | Any value you also guard with the protocol byte; the researcher recalled 0xFFFF as SIG-reserved and 0x02E5 as Espressif's but did not verify either |

Build the buffer directly and skip `ble_gap_adv_set_fields`; on receive call `ble_hs_adv_find_field(BLE_HS_ADV_TYPE_MFG_DATA, desc->data, desc->length_data, &f)` and check `f->length`, the company ID and the protocol byte before trusting a byte ([ble_hs_adv.h:230-237](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/nimble/nimble/host/include/host/ble_hs_adv.h)). Copy what you need inside the callback: `desc->data` points into the HCI event buffer.

### Scanning, peer table, expiry and the mix

Scan with `ble_gap_disc(own_addr_type, BLE_HS_FOREVER, &p, cb, NULL)` where `p = { .itvl = 0xA0, .window = 0xA0, .passive = 1, .filter_duplicates = 0, .limited = 0 }`, i.e. **100 ms window = interval**; try 50 ms as the alternative in the measurements. `filter_duplicates` must stay 0 with the controller's default "by device" filter, which reports a given address once per scan session and never refreshes with `DUPL_SCAN_CACHE_REFRESH_PERIOD = 0`; the later optimisation is `CONFIG_BT_CTRL_SCAN_DUPL_TYPE_DATA_DEVICE=y` plus `filter_duplicates = 1`, which suppresses identical repeats in the controller and passes only changed data, at the cost of hiding the repeats that liveness relies on ([esp32c3/Kconfig.in:266-334](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/controller/esp32c3/Kconfig.in)). The callback posts a 16-byte record to a queue; a render task owns a peer table of up to 16 entries keyed by address with the last state, last `seq` and last receive tick, and treats its own state as one more entry so the holder's own colour is mixed exactly like a peer's. Release is explicit: every event after the finger lifts carries `holding = 0`, so peers clear within interval + 10 ms. Expiry covers only a dead or out-of-range peer and is **3 × steady interval + 100 ms**: 250 ms at a fixed 20 ms (about twelve consecutive misses, 0.24¹² ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁸ in the idealised ten-board model), 400 ms once the 100 ms steady state exists. A 100 ms fade on add and remove hides the remaining jitter, as the badge project's 50 ms quantisation did.

Mix in linear light, not sRGB, and sum rather than average: a plain per-channel sum must be clamped (what FastLED's saturating `qadd8` and `CRGB +=` do, [FastLED wiki](https://github.com/FastLED/FastLED/wiki/High-performance-math)), averaging halves brightness per holder, and blending in gamma-encoded sRGB produces dark midpoints that linear blending removes ([OBS wiki](https://obsproject.com/wiki/Linear-Color-Blending)). The default that matches "two torches on one wall" at a fixed display maximum is sum then divide by the largest channel so the maximum hits full scale, the normalisation a graphics patent prefers over plain summation that "breaks down when more than a few light sources are present" ([US 6018350](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6018350)); red plus green gives full yellow, red plus blue full magenta, all three white. Never average hue in HSV; it needs a circular mean and still has no answer for opposite hues ([iter.ca](https://iter.ca/post/hue-avg/)). Two 256-entry LUTs and 16-bit accumulators do this in integer math per frame.

### The firmware link

The central (serving board) opens the connection with `itvl_min = itvl_max = 6` (**7.5 ms**), latency 0, supervision timeout 600 (6 s), `min_ce_len = 12`, `max_ce_len = 24` (7.5/15 ms), the values of Espressif's throughput example ([Kconfig.projbuild:9-52](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/throughput_app/blecent_throughput/main/Kconfig.projbuild)); in its connect handler it runs, in order, `ble_hs_hci_util_set_data_len(conn, 251, 2120)`, `ble_att_set_preferred_mtu(512)`, `ble_gattc_exchange_mtu()` and `ble_gap_update_params()` ([blecent main.c:787-816](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/throughput_app/blecent_throughput/main/main.c)), then requests 2M with `ble_gap_set_prefered_le_phy(conn, BLE_HCI_LE_PHY_2M_PREF_MASK, BLE_HCI_LE_PHY_2M_PREF_MASK, 0)` and waits for `BLE_GAP_EVENT_MTU`, `BLE_GAP_EVENT_DATA_LEN_CHG` and `BLE_GAP_EVENT_PHY_UPDATE_COMPLETE` before streaming; the MTU is not exchanged automatically ([#12789](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/12789)). Framing copies Espressif's `ble_ota` protocol without depending on the component: 509-byte writes (MTU − 3) carrying a 2-byte sector index, a 1-byte packet sequence and 506 data bytes, the last packet of each 4 KB sector tagged 0xFF with the sector's CRC16 in its last two bytes, a 20-byte per-sector ACK with status from the receiver, and a sender window of 4 sectors so no ACK round trip (≥ 7.5 ms, 512 of them for 2 MB) stalls the pipe ([ble_ota README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/esp-iot-solution/master/examples/bluetooth/ble_ota/README.md)). `BLE_HS_ENOMEM` from the mbuf pool means back off one tick and retry, never data loss ([blecent_throughput README:142](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/bluetooth/nimble/throughput_app/blecent_throughput/README.md)).

The sender serves exactly `esp_image_get_metadata(&pos, &md).image_len` bytes of its running partition, which includes the appended SHA-256 ([esp_image_format.h:28-38, 116-128](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bootloader_support/include/esp_image_format.h), [esp_image_format.c:969-975](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bootloader_support/src/esp_image_format.c)), read through `esp_partition_mmap()` so that no SPI1 transaction stalls the caches ([esp_partition.h:372-396](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/esp_partition/include/esp_partition.h)), and puts `image_len` and `md.image_digest` in the START command; it serves only while `esp_ota_get_state_partition(running)` is `ESP_OTA_IMG_VALID` (or not found, for a factory image). The receiver checks the first 288 bytes before writing anything: `esp_app_desc_t` sits at image offset 32, so `magic_word == 0xABCD5432`, equal `project_name`, strictly higher semantic version (IDF ships no comparator; write one, with `-rcN` ranking below the release) and a version different from `esp_ota_get_last_invalid_partition()`'s ([app_image_format.rst:92-108](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-reference/system/app_image_format.rst), [native_ota_example.c:143-196](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/system/ota/native_ota_example/main/native_ota_example.c)). It then runs `esp_ota_begin(part, OTA_WITH_SEQUENTIAL_WRITES, &h)` so each sector is erased just before it is written, feeds 4 KB sectors from a PSRAM ring buffer into `esp_ota_write` from a dedicated task (never from the GATT callback), and finishes with `esp_ota_end`, which verifies magic, segments, checksum and the appended SHA-256, then `esp_ota_set_boot_partition` and restart ([esp_ota_ops.c:314-335, 457-461](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/app_update/esp_ota_ops.c), [esp_image_format.h:100-106](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bootloader_support/include/esp_image_format.h)). With `CONFIG_BOOTLOADER_APP_ROLLBACK_ENABLE=y` the new image boots in `PENDING_VERIFY` and has exactly one boot to call `esp_ota_mark_app_valid_cancel_rollback()` after its own self-test (display up, host synced, flood running); a still-pending image cannot even start another download (`ESP_ERR_OTA_ROLLBACK_INVALID_STATE`) and, by the serve rule above, does not serve ([Kconfig.app_rollback:3-13](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bootloader/Kconfig.app_rollback), [esp_ota_ops.c:165-170](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/app_update/esp_ota_ops.c), [ota.rst:85-120](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-reference/system/ota.rst)). Power loss mid-transfer is harmless because the running slot and the two-sector otadata are untouched until the end ([ota.rst:13-34](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-reference/system/ota.rst)). Expect **~34 s for 2 MB at the documented 500 kbps** on 1M PHY and 12-26 s on 2M (the #12789 closing comment reports 750-794 kbps at 1M and 1.33-1.38 Mbps at 2M with the same example; an S3 forum snippet reports 46 KB/s stock and 102.5 KB/s on 2M with logging off, [esp32.com p=134183](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=134183)), plus about 3 s of flash writing at the 0.647 MB/s raw-write benchmark figure ([perf_benchmark README:182-183](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/examples/storage/perf_benchmark/README.md)). During every `esp_ota_write` the other core busy-waits and non-IRAM interrupts are off, and DMA reads from flash during erase or write return unpredictable data, so any LVGL asset the display DMA reads must already be in PSRAM or SRAM ([spi_flash_concurrency.rst:47, 85](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-reference/peripherals/spi_flash/spi_flash_concurrency.rst)); `CONFIG_SPI_FLASH_WRITE_CHUNK_SIZE=4096` halves each cache-off window ([spi_flash Kconfig:345-353](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/spi_flash/Kconfig)).

### Budget and Kconfig

The controller allocates everything from `MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL | MALLOC_CAP_DMA` and needs "over 20 KB" of IRAM that cannot move ([bt.c:137](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/controller/esp32c3/bt.c), [esp32c3/Kconfig.in:521-530](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/controller/esp32c3/Kconfig.in)); the NimBLE host's pools are about 21 KB at defaults (30 KB with `MSYS_1_BLOCK_COUNT=50`) and can move wholesale to PSRAM with `CONFIG_BT_NIMBLE_MEM_ALLOC_MODE_EXTERNAL`, which this project can select because its build already has `CONFIG_SPIRAM_USE_MALLOC=y` ([NimBLE Kconfig.in:2-35](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/components/bt/host/nimble/Kconfig.in), [project sdkconfig:1235](file:///C:/Users/lmathieu/ESPDEV/esp32-s3-rtcquebec/build/v55/sdkconfig)). The researcher's estimate for advertising plus scanning plus two connections is **roughly 55-75 KB of internal SRAM**, less with host pools in PSRAM; the only S3 data point is a ~45 KB free-heap drop at NimBLE init under NimBLE-Arduino defaults ([NimBLE-Arduino #1046](https://github.com/h2zero/NimBLE-Arduino/issues/1046)). Those figures were computed against ~100 KB free with the Wi-Fi driver resident; a Wi-Fi-free build has more, unquantified (the coexistence guide's memory list names ~17 KB of IRAM for `ESP_WIFI_RX_IRAM_OPT` alone, [coexist.rst:267-288](file:///C:/Espressif/esp-idf/docs/en/api-guides/coexist.rst)). ## Eight risks to measure before building on it

Everything above that is a number rather than a quotation is either a model or a figure from other silicon, because Espressif publishes no measured advertising loss, scan-channel rotation, notification latency or NimBLE footprint for the ESP32-S3, and the controller is closed source. The first week on the three boards should therefore be instrumentation, in the order below; the first three decide whether the flood holds at ten boards, the next three whether the firmware link is viable with the display running, and the last two whether it feels right.

## Conclusion

The research collapses what looked like three competing network technologies into one physical mechanism plus two kinds of wrapper. SIG mesh and the custom flood both put state into non-connectable advertisements received by continuously scanning peers; the mesh stack adds encryption, replay protection and a provisioning model built for phones and buildings, and in exchange takes away the payload, the sending rate and the GATT server this project needs for firmware. The decision is therefore not about latency, where the two are equal, but about what you have to build around the radio: nothing for the flood, a provisioner and a workaround for mesh. GATT connections sit on the other side of the same line: they are the right tool the moment bytes must arrive in order and in bulk, which is exactly the firmware case and exactly not the colour case, and NimBLE lets one application hold both roles at once on the S3 without the activity budget or the vendor scheduler becoming a constraint.

Two implications follow for what comes after the proof of concept. First, treating the display as a stateless renderer of a shared snapshot, as PixMob and the badge swarm do, means effects grow by adding parameters to a packet that already has fifteen spare bytes, not by adding bandwidth, and a shared clock makes ten boards animate in step without any synchronisation protocol. Second, the design that is least sensitive to a Wi-Fi station returning later is this one, because nothing is acknowledged and nothing times out; but every latency figure in this report was derived for an empty radio, and the notes' coexistence evidence (50/50 slicing, one in N advertising events, a reported jump from 3% to 20% loss) says that adding Wi-Fi reopens risks 2 and 3 in full and makes the 20 ms burst, not the 100 ms steady state, the thing that keeps the toy feeling instant.
